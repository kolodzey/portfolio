Roots in Art and Design

Art was my first way of exploring ideas and feelings that were often difficult to put into words. That connection eventually led me to study Product Design, where I became deeply interested in the relationship between an idea and the material used to create it.

During my studies, the Bauhaus philosophy shaped the way I learned to think about design: to create something meaningful, you first need to understand its materials, behaviours, and processes. I experienced this most directly through glassmaking: learning to shape the material with my own hands, understanding its possibilities and limitations before translating that knowledge into design.

That mindset has stayed with me. Whether I’m working with physical materials, interfaces, design systems, or code, I want to understand what I’m working with before deciding what it should become.

Finding the space between design and engineering

My career moved between software development and UX/UI design for several years before I realised that I didn’t need to choose between them.

Development taught me to think in systems, structure, constraints, and behaviour. Design taught me to think about people, interaction, intention, and the experience as a whole. The more I worked in both disciplines, the more naturally they began to inform one another.

Today, I’m most at home in the space between them: moving from product questions into interaction design, design systems, prototyping, and implementation without treating those steps as isolated handoffs.

How I work today

I do my best work when I can understand the whole problem and take meaningful ownership of it. I like shaping direction, making ideas tangible, testing them in real interfaces, and refining both the experience and the system behind it.

I’m especially interested in design–development workflows, design systems, and the ways AI is changing how products are designed and built. For me, the most interesting work often happens not within one discipline, but in the connections between them.
