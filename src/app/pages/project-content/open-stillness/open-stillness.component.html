Why I created it

Open Stillness began with a very practical need: I wanted one calm place to collect the practices I kept returning to. What started as a small personal tool for organizing Yin routines gradually became something much more meaningful.

As I continued working with meditation, breathwork, and gentle movement, the project changed with me. I became less interested in simply storing practices and more interested in shaping the experience around them: creating a space that makes slowing down feel intuitive, inviting, and easy to return to.

From a small personal tool to an evolving product

Open Stillness did not begin as a platform. It started as a small personal project and gradually changed as my own practice, ideas, and ambitions for the experience grew.

2023

A personal starting point

I began with 16 hand-drawn Yin poses and a small Angular application to organize routines I was using myself.

2024

Starting again

As the idea grew, the original concept no longer felt right. I discarded the first design and application and rebuilt Open Stillness from scratch with a clearer product structure, stronger visual language, and room to expand beyond Yin practice.

Today

A seasonal product

Open Stillness now brings together Meditation, Breathwork, and Yin Stretching through seasonal experiences, supported by hand-drawn illustration, animation, original sound, and guided video.

Designing a product system

Open Stillness is built around a shared structure of Meditation, Breathwork, and Yin Stretching. Within that structure, practices are organized into routines, poses, reflections, and guided experiences that can be combined in different ways without changing how the product fundamentally works.

The seasonal model gives the platform room to evolve without losing consistency. Each season can introduce its own typography, color palette, imagery, atmosphere, sound, and emotional tone, while the underlying components and interaction patterns remain familiar.

This allows Open Stillness to feel different from season to season without becoming a different product each time. The system gives me enough structure to scale the platform, while still leaving space for experimentation, storytelling, and a distinct sense of place.

Creating the experience end to end

Open Stillness brings together many different layers of work, and I shape all of them as part of one product experience. That includes the product structure, interface design, design system, frontend implementation, accessibility, and the visual language that gives each season its character.

I also create the content that lives inside the product: hundreds of hand-drawn pose illustrations, animated practice visuals, original sound, atmospheric environments, and guided videos. Because these elements are designed alongside the interface rather than added afterward, they can support the same mood, rhythm, and interaction principles as the product itself.

Working across these layers gives me a different kind of ownership. I can follow an idea from an early product decision through visual design, interaction, implementation, and content. And I can refine the experience as a whole rather than as a series of separate handoffs.

The product today

Open Stillness continues to evolve through new seasons, practices, and experiences. The interface stays familiar while each season brings its own atmosphere and visual character.

Building and evolving it

Open Stillness is built as an Angular application with TypeScript and Tailwind CSS, using reusable components and responsive patterns that allow the platform to grow without rebuilding each experience from scratch. Accessibility, cross-browser behaviour, and responsive design are part of the implementation process rather than something added at the end.

The product also moves beyond the interface itself. I use Figma to shape the product and design system, Procreate for the hand-drawn visual language and animation work, and Ableton to create and arrange the soundscapes that accompany the practices. As Open Stillness evolves, I continue to refine both the experience and the systems behind it.
